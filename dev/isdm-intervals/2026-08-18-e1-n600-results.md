0 INDETERMINATE.** The pre-declared gate required ≥ 90 % PASS; this is 100 %.

## 2. Final verdicts

`e1-final-verdicts.csv` (48 rows, one per cell × species — never pooled).

- **PASS 48 / 48.**
- Point coverage range **0.939 – 0.959**; all 48 inside [0.90, 0.98] and all
  48 inside the stricter [0.92, 0.98] decision band.
- Median Wilson half-width **0.0090** (0.0429 at n = 100 in the feasibility
  run, 0.0166 at n = 600) — the resolution gain the escalation was for.
- **K3 alarms (coverage < 0.80): 0.**
- PD-Hessian rate 0.858 – 0.947.

**Reading.** The uncalibrated Wald SE on the `trait:env` fixed effect is
calibrated across this grid. Coverage sits slightly *below* the 0.95 nominal
throughout (0.939–0.959, centred near 0.947) — a mild, consistent
under-coverage that is nonetheless inside the pre-declared band everywhere.
No calibrator is needed for this estimand on this grid.

## 3. Why 14 verdicts escalated

At n = 600 the 14 INDETERMINATE cases were all the same shape: coverage
0.924–0.938 with the Wilson lower bound just *below* the 0.92 band floor.
Nothing was failing; the interval simply straddled the boundary. Nine of the
16 cells were affected and were escalated to n = 2000 exactly as the
pre-registration's rule specifies — the other seven were left at n = 600,
rather than uniformly escalating everything, so the executed design matches
the registered one.

## 4. 🔴 An unexplained systematic difference between seed blocks

Reported because it is real and was measured, not because it changes the
conclusion.

Comparing the two seed blocks on the nine escalated cells:

| block | seeds | coverage (pooled over the 9 cells) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1001–1600 | sp1 0.9417 · sp2 0.9409 · sp3 0.9383 |
| 2 | 1601–3000 | sp1 0.9475 · sp2 0.9480 · sp3 0.9556 |

Per cell × species, **21 of 27 differences are positive**, mean **+0.0101**.
Under a 50/50 null that sign pattern has p ≈ 0.004, so it is systematic
rather than sampling noise. **I have no mechanism for it.** The data-
generating code re-seeds per replicate and nothing in it is obviously
seed-dependent beyond the covariate draw.

**It does not threaten the verdict.** Block 1 alone gives 0.938–0.942, still
above the 0.92 floor, so every verdict is PASS under either block
separately as well as combined. But it is a live anomaly in a campaign whose
whole purpose is calibration, and it should be looked at before this grid is
reused — a coverage estimate that depends on the seed block is exactly the
kind of thing that turns into a false finding later.

Note also that the table above is a **pooled** quantity, which this campaign
forbids for verdicts. It is used here only to describe the anomaly, never to
support the PASS.

## 5. Scope — what this does and does not license

**Licensed:** a narrow `covered` note for *this estimand* (`trait:env` Wald
coverage) on *this grid* (`n_cells` {150, 810} × `eff_ratio` {1, 10} ×
`n_sources` {2, 3} × amplitude {1×, 2×}), at nominal 95 %, on PD-Hessian fits.

**NOT licensed, explicitly:**

- Any claim about **E2** (latent-loading amplitude — never ADREPORTed with an
  SE), **E3** (intensity ratios — not computable, no row-pair covariance is
  exposed), or **E4** (`predict(se.fit)` eta coverage — measured *negative*
  in the feasibility run at 0.23–0.82 and **not** re-litigated here).
- Anything outside the grid's factor ranges.
- Anything about non-PD fits: 5–14 % of fits per cell have no usable Wald
  interval and are excluded from the denominator, never imputed. The
  *availability* of an interval is a separate question from its coverage, and
  only the latter is answered here.
- Any map-scale or prediction interval. #1133 item 4 stands unchanged.
