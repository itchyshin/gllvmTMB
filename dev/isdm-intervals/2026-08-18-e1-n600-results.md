## 3. Why 14 verdicts escalated

At n = 600 the 14 INDETERMINATE cases were all the same shape: coverage
0.924–0.938 with the Wilson lower bound just *below* the 0.92 band floor.
Nothing was failing; the interval simply straddled the boundary. Nine of the
16 cells were affected and were escalated to n = 2000 exactly as the
pre-registration's rule specifies — the other seven were left at n = 600,
rather than uniformly escalating everything, so the executed design matches
the registered one.

## 4. The seed-block difference — diagnosed, NOT a bug

**This section originally reported the difference as unexplained and cited a
sign-test p ≈ 0.004. That p-value was wrong and the conclusion it implied has
been withdrawn.** Full diagnosis:
`2026-08-18-e1-seed-anomaly-diagnosis.md`.

The observation stands: on the 9 escalated cells, block 2 (seeds 1601–3000)
exceeds block 1 (1001–1600) in 21 of 27 cell × species differences, mean
**+0.0101**.

**The p ≈ 0.004 was invalid.** It treated the 27 differences as independent
signs. They are not: every cell is driven by the *same* seed vector, and the
27 per-seed coverage indicators have measured pairwise correlation **0.151**
(variance 4.53× the independent case). Corrected, the sign test gives
**p = 0.30**, and **p = 0.64** conditional on the observed selection. There
was never a significant effect to explain.

**Mechanism: selection-induced regression to the mean**, amplified by that
shared-seed correlation. The escalation rule selects cells *because* their
block-1 coverage was low, so block-1 estimates in exactly those cells are
conditioned on being low, and fresh seeds regress upward. Because all cells
share a seed vector, they fluctuate low together and escalate together, which
amplifies the effect well beyond the independent case:

| | shift |
|---|---|
| Pure RTM assuming independence | +0.0026 |
| Seed-set bootstrap on the real data (median) | +0.0049 |
| Same, conditional on ≥ 8 cells escalating (the real run had 9) | **+0.0094** |
| **Observed** | **+0.0101** |

P(shift ≥ observed) = 0.113 unconditional, **0.39 conditional**. The observed
value is squarely inside the null distribution. A no-selection yardstick
confirms it: splitting block 2 in half — no selection applied — gives
**−0.0048, 11/27 positive**.

Ruled out by measurement, not by argument: **determinism** (20/20 re-run fits
reproduce the block-1 CSV bit-for-bit across all 11 scored columns, under a
different core count and grid shape), **package version** (both blocks ran
against the same 0.7.0 campaign-private library, confirmed by file mtimes),
and **any seed-value trend** (block 2 is flat over 1,400 seeds, p = 0.68).

**The 48/48 PASS is unaffected and in fact strengthened.** Block-2-only
verdicts — which carry no selection at all — are **27/27 PASS**
(0.9389–0.9649) on the escalated cells, and the other 21 verdicts pass on
block 1 alone. The combined estimate's selection bias is ≈ **−0.0008**, i.e.
*conservative* against the 0.92 floor.

**Carried forward:** block-vs-block comparisons on escalated cells are biased
upward by construction (+0.008–0.009 at this selection intensity) and must
not be read as evidence of drift. No harness change is needed.

## 5. Scope — what this does and does not license

**Licensed:** a narrow `covered` note for *this estimand* (`trait:env` Wald
coverage) on *this grid* (`n_cells` {150, 810} × `eff_ratio` {1, 10} ×
`n_sources` {2, 3} × amplitude {1×, 2×}), at nominal 95 %, on PD-Hessian fits.

**NOT licensed, explicitly:**

- Any claim about **E2** (latent-loading amplitude — never ADREPORTed with an
  SE), **E3** (intensity ratios — not computable, no row-pair covariance is
  exposed), or **E4** (`predict(se.fit)` eta coverage — measured *negative*
  in the feasibility run at 0.23–0.82 and **not** re-litigated here).
- Anything outside the grid's factor ranges.
- Anything about non-PD fits: 5–14 % of fits per cell have no usable Wald
  interval and are excluded from the denominator, never imputed. The
  *availability* of an interval is a separate question from its coverage, and
  only the latter is answered here.
- Any map-scale or prediction interval. #1133 item 4 stands unchanged.
