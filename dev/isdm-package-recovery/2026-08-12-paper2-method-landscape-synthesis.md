## Grounded conclusions

Kristensen et al. supports the narrow computational statement that TMB obtains
derivatives by automatic differentiation and uses a Laplace approximation for
random effects. Thus objective, gradient, Hessian, convergence code, and
memory/time stage receipts are appropriate *diagnostic records* for the fixed
TMB estimator. It does **not** endorse this programme's raw-gradient cutoff,
Case-C classification, retry policy, or any recovery threshold.

Hui et al. supports the general methodological point that latent-factor
components can represent residual association and that latent dimensions need
interpretation/identifiability care. It makes the allocation between rank-one
`Lambda Lambda'` and diagonal `Psi` a legitimate object to inspect. It does
**not** establish that a mixed Poisson/cloglog, shared-state model has
identified Psi at S = 6, 20, or 60, nor that a particular Psi error is caused
by numerical non-admission.

Morris et al. supports separating the data-generating mechanism, estimands,
methods, performance measures, and targets. In this programme that means
separating all-attempt numerical admission from all-attempt Psi recovery and
reporting their joint table, rather than conditioning recovery on eligible
fits. It does **not** supply a pass cutoff or validate this iJSDM design.

## Consequence for the private design

The literature permits neither a transfer of the existing boundary-only
Case-B polish to non-boundary `b_fix`/`theta_rr_B` Case C nor a claim that a
raw-gradient state explains Psi error. The admissible A3 design response is
therefore: retain Case C as `NO_CANDIDATE`; pre-register S = 6, 20, 60
all-attempt joint numerical/Psi summaries; and treat any observed association
as descriptive. No source supports spatial, count-survey, occupancy/detection,
empirical, absolute-abundance, generic-zero-inflation, arbitrary-source, or
S = 10,000 claims.
